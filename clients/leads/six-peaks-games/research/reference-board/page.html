A creative AI studio in Oslo whose front page is a playable pixel-art town. It is a services studio, not a game studio, which is the point: it does a services studio's jobs as a game.

Signature idea

Every business job is a place. References are clients you walk up to, the service line is a sign, contact is the post office, and a MET 0/7 counter makes reading references the goal. The way out to the traditional site never leaves the screen.

What to steal for Six Peaks

  • The nine partner testimonials become people you meet on the route, each with the studio's real logo and the quote, name and role verbatim. A MET 0/9 counter sets the goal.
  • Book a trial becomes a place: walking in opens the same inline form as the readable layer, with email and phone as copyable text.
  • The traditional site becomes the manual: pillars, services, all 17 titles with partner credit, team, careers and contact as plain HTML under the game, complete at rest.

Proposed new Direction A · replaces The Ascent

Bug Hunt

The site is a test build you play.

You walk the six peaks of the Six Peaks mark, log the bugs we planted, meet the partners, and reach the summit, where your run report sits next to Book a trial. Everything the game shows is also in a plain manual under it.

Home route, and how it differs from Press Start

A · Bug Hunt

#home

Axis: one run you play, title to summit. Content is found in the world and mirrored in the manual.

  1. Title screen. The Six Peaks logo and the live H1, Beyond Standard QA, with QA as the start control. The live sub line, We live and breathe your games with you. Two doors: Start the test run and Book a trial. Silent by default, the nine platform icons as Tested on, and a link to the manual.
  2. Rules. Find the eight bugs, meet the nine partners, reach the sixth peak. The six pillars as six power-ups, verbatim. The bug bestiary: eight glyphs, one per service. Controls for keys and touch.
  3. The run. One canvas level whose skyline is the Six Peaks mark, one pillar per peak. HUD: LOGGED, MET, time. The 13 shipped titles are landmarks that open cards with partner credit; the 4 underway titles are boarded signs; nine partners stand on the route with their quotes; team photos are pickups.
  4. Bug report. Each log files a card: title, steps, expected, actual, severity, and the service that catches it, quoted from the live site. The level gets one polish pass per log.
  5. Summit. Run report (bugs logged, partners met, time) over the rooftop team photo with the range behind. “Ready for a real build?” with the Book a trial form and copyable email and phone.
  6. Manual. The readable layer, always rendered under the game: what we offer, six pillars, eight services, 15 partners, 17 titles with partner credit, team, careers in George, ISO 27001 adherence, contact.

B · Press Start

Approved · frozen

Axis: a catalogue you browse. Titles lead, each one opens a dossier.

  1. Showcase hero. Rotating key art under the tester's view overlay.
  2. Tested on.
  3. Library (17). Poster grid, each tile opens a QA dossier route.
  4. Loadout (8). Services as equipment slots.
  5. Co-op mode.
  6. Reviews (9).
  7. Credits.
  8. Crew.
  9. Press start. Contact.
  • A has no key-art hero, no carousel, no library grid and no dossier pages. Titles are landmarks you reach on the route.
  • Services: B shows them as equipment you pick; A shows each one as the bug it catches.
  • Partners: B collects reviews in a hub; A puts each quote in the mouth of a partner you meet.
  • Ending: B closes on its contact section; A closes on a summit that only opens after your run report.
  • Reading: B's page is its own reading path; A keeps a separate manual that is complete without playing.

Signature mechanic: log the bug

  1. Spot. Eight faults are planted in the Six Peaks level, one for each service on the live site.
  2. Log. Press Space or tap the fault. A bug report files itself: title, steps, expected, actual, severity.
  3. Prove. The report names the Six Peaks service that catches this kind of bug and quotes it from the live site.
  4. Ship. Each log applies one polish pass to the level: light, colour, depth. The eighth log turns the build gold and opens the summit.

Real assets, and where they go

  • Six Peaks markThe level's skyline and its six checkpoints.
  • 13 shipped titles: key art, posters, logosLandmarks that open in-game cards with partner credit and the Key Testing Focus lines verbatim.
  • 4 underway titlesBoarded signs with no art: “Project underway, further details on release”.
  • 9 testimonials and 15 partner logosPartners you meet on the route, quotes verbatim with name, role and studio.
  • 8 servicesThe bug bestiary and the report cards.
  • 6 pillarsPower-ups on the rules screen and signs on the peaks.
  • Team photos and 4 headshotsPickups on the route and the summit backdrop.
  • 9 platform iconsThe Tested on row on the title screen.

Needs a ruling before build

  • Planted bugs vs “never invent bugs”.The brief bans invented bugs. These are demo faults in our own level, labelled as a demo, never attached to a partner title and never presented as findings. The line manager decides.
  • Who draws the level.We draw it, in line or pixel form, from the logo silhouette. No fake game art; partner art appears only inside title cards, credited.
  • The game stays optional.The manual is complete at rest, keys and touch both work, reduced motion gets a static level map with buttons, sound is off until asked, and there is no turn-your-phone gate.
